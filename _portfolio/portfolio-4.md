## Project Overview
Drought is a natural phenomenon whose impacts can be significantly mitigated through consistent and accurate monitoring. This research, conducted for my B.Sc. in Civil-Geomatics Engineering at K. N. Toosi University of Technology, aimed to investigate and quantify the drought situation across Iran. By leveraging remote sensing techniques, the project extracted spatial information with high accuracy and minimal cost, mapping drought dynamics over a comprehensive 20-year period (1999–2019).

## Methodology & Remote Sensing Pipeline
The core of this project involved processing large volumes of **MODIS** satellite imagery, specifically focusing on vegetation cover and Land Surface Temperature (LST). I calculated and synthesized four key environmental indices to generate monthly drought maps:

* **Vegetation Condition Index (VCI):** Compared current Normalized Difference Vegetation Index (NDVI) values to historical minimum and maximum ranges to identify vegetation stress.
* **Temperature Condition Index (TCI):** Estimated vegetation stress caused by extreme temperatures and wetness, utilizing maximum and minimum LSTs.
* **Vegetation Health Index (VHI):** Derived from a combination of LST and NDVI to illustrate the overall severity of drought based on both plant health and thermal influence.
* **Standard Vegetation Index (SVI):** Provided a relative temporal comparison of vegetation conditions to assess long-term drought trends.

## Validation & Key Findings
* **Index Consistency:** Cross-comparison among the four derived indicators (VCI, TCI, VHI, and SVI) demonstrated high compatibility, confirming the reliability of the processed MODIS data with no major spatial discrepancies.
* **Ground-Truth Validation:** To evaluate the accuracy of the satellite-derived results, the generated maps for 2014 were validated against the Standardized Precipitation Index (SPI) drought maps produced by the National Meteorological Organization. 
* **Conclusion:** The remote sensing pipeline yielded highly acceptable results, with the satellite-derived drought maps showing strong consistency with both the SPI index and the established climatic conditions of Iran's diverse regions.
