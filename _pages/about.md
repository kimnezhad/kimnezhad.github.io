<div style="text-align: justify;">

I am a Remote Sensing Researcher specializing in the intersection of cryospheric science, multi-sensor remote sensing, and scalable machine learning. I hold an M.Sc. in Geomatics – Remote Sensing Engineering and a B.Sc. in Civil – Geomatics Engineering from K. N. Toosi University of Technology. Currently, I serve as a Remote Research Collaborator with Brown University (advised by Dr. Christopher Horvat), where I lead computational research on Antarctic and Arctic sea-ice classification. 

My research focuses on developing robust data engineering pipelines, deep learning architectures, and data fusion techniques to monitor sea-ice dynamics, quantify observational uncertainties, and improve polar climate monitoring. Operating autonomously across international collaborations, my work integrates high-resolution Sentinel-1 SAR imagery, passive microwave retrievals, and ICESat-2 laser altimetry. I design end-to-end processing pipelines—scaling up to multi-million-tile datasets—to train advanced neural networks and ensemble models for automated sea-ice concentration and stage-of-development mapping.

Beyond SAR, my foundational expertise spans geospatial analysis, spatial modeling, optical image processing, and signal analysis. Prior to my dedicated focus on the cryosphere, I applied multi-temporal satellite analysis and machine learning to complex environmental health and land-cover challenges. This background has equipped me with highly transferable skills in data science, spatial statistics, satellite data processing, computer vision, and multi-sensor data fusion for comprehensive environmental monitoring.

I am actively seeking Ph.D. opportunities in cryosphere remote sensing and Earth observation, where I can leverage my advanced computational skills to address pressing geophysical and climate challenges.
