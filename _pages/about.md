I am an Earth Observation and Remote Sensing researcher specializing in the intersection of cryospheric science, radar remote sensing, and scalable machine learning. My research focuses on developing robust data engineering pipelines and deep learning architectures to monitor sea-ice dynamics, quantify observational uncertainties, and improve polar climate monitoring.
Currently, I serve as a Remote Research Collaborator with Brown University (advised by Dr. Christopher Horvat), where I lead computational research on Antarctic and Arctic sea-ice characterization. Operating autonomously across international collaborations, my work integrates high-resolution Sentinel-1 SAR imagery, passive microwave retrievals, and ICESat-2 laser altimetry. I design end-to-end processing pipelines—scaling up to multi-million-tile datasets—to train Convolutional Neural Networks (ResNets, U-Nets) and gradient-boosted decision trees for automated sea-ice concentration and stage-of-development mapping.

I hold an M.Sc. in Geomatics – Remote Sensing Engineering (GPA: 3.77/4.00) and a B.Sc. in Civil – Geomatics Engineering from K. N. Toosi University of Technology. My foundational training spans geodesy, spatial modeling, and polarimetric radar analysis. Prior to my dedicated focus on the cryosphere, I applied multi-temporal satellite analysis and machine learning to complex environmental health and land-cover challenges, building transferable expertise in spatial statistics, sensor calibration, and computer vision.

I am actively seeking competitive Ph.D. opportunities in cryosphere remote sensing, Earth Observation, and spatial data assimilation, where I can leverage my advanced computational skills to address pressing geophysical and climate challenges.
